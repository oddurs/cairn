# Roadmap

Project memory, versioned with the code. Plain Markdown, a schema you own, and a shared workflow for people and agents.

Cairn keeps intent, work, and decisions beside the code. Harrow is the terminal
interface for watching that work and deciding what needs attention.

The next outcome is **a daily workflow we can trust across people, agents, and
Git branches**. Then we make the compatibility promise of 1.0. Releases follow
evidence, not placeholder dates. The assessment and design boundaries are in
[0134](cairn/items/0134-give-cairn-a-durable-direction-and-a-working-project-setup.md).

This page shows unfinished work. `planned` means selected for the next cycle;
`backlog` means an option; `blocked` means an explicit wait. Dependencies are
recorded separately. The `later` milestone is not a release commitment.

Use `cairn list --view next` or `harrow --view next` to choose work,
`cairn list --view waiting` for external actions, and
`cairn list --view history` or `cairn search --all TEXT` for the record of past
decisions. Closed items stay in Git; they are omitted here to keep the next
steps readable.
